    regulatory approval from the **People's Bank of China (PBOC)** to establish
    and operate a domestic bank card clearing business within mainland China.
    Historically, domestic currency clearing in China has remained largely
    insulated from foreign card schemes, leading institutional market models to
    treat full domestic market access as a low-probability, long-duration
    option. Consequently, the PBOC's official clearance acted as a high-impact
    catalyst that dismantled a multi-year geopolitical and market-access
    barrier, prompting an immediate recalibration of Visa's long-term total
    addressable payment volume (TAM).


    This regulatory announcement exhibited clear fundamental alignment
    (**ALIGNED**), further reinforced by positive commercial and analytical
    tailwinds. In parallel with the China clearing revelation, Wall Street
    research desks adjusted projections—highlighted by BMO raising its price

    transition of stablecoin-powered settlement pilots into live production
    environments. While pre-session sentiment was insulated by conservative
    expectations, the sudden expansion of addressable volume sparked rapid price
    discovery. However, an analytical expectations gap persists: while the
    equity market swiftly factored in total addressable growth, physical market
    penetration within China requires extensive technological integration,
    compliance validation, and cohabitation with dominant domestic payment rails
    (China UnionPay, Alipay, WeChat Pay), indicating that revenue contribution
    will materialize across an extended corporate cycle rather than in immediate
    quarterly prints.
